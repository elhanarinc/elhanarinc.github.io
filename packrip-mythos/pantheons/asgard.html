What you'll rip in Asgard

Asgard is the snow-and-iron counterpart to Olympus. The same fixed rarity split — 25 Common, 15 Uncommon, 10 Rare, 5 Holo Rare, 3 Holo EX, 1 Shining, 1 Gold Star — but the casting leans on Norse iconography: animal-companion deities, named weapons, named beasts of Ragnarök.

Highlight chase cards

  • Odin — Secret variant. Spectral type. The pantheon's flagship Secret pull (~5%); painted with Huginn and Muninn at his shoulders.
  • Thor — Holo EX. Storm type. Mjölnir mid-swing, lightning chasing the holo plate.
  • Loki — Gold Star. Void type. The set's rarest standard-tier pull at ~2.8%.
  • Fenrir — Shining. Bone type. The bound wolf, snow drifting against ivory holo.
  • Jörmungandr — Holo EX. Abyssal type. The world serpent, scales catching deep teal.
  • Freyja — Holo Rare. Verdant type. Falcon cloak, Brísingamen at the throat.

The 9-energy split

Norse myth weights heavily toward Storm (Thor and the storm-giants), Bone (Hel, Fenrir, the einherjar), and Titan (the jötnar). Solar and Aether feature less, reflecting the colder cosmology.

How the artwork was made

Same fal.ai pipeline as Olympus: prompts written by hand from public-domain Eddic source material, rendered through Flux models in an oil-painting style, then bundled into the app. Filler tiers used Flux Schnell; chase tiers Flux Dev. The full pantheon cost roughly $0.33. The visual identity skews colder — more snow, more iron, more ravens, more deep blues than the gold of Olympus.

Public-domain Eddic sources only. Asgard draws on Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the Poetic Edda, sagas like Völsunga, and other public-domain texts. The renders are interpretations, not reproductions of any contemporary illustrator's work.
